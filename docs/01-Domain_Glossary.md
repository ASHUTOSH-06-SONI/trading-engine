# Domain Glossary

1. **Order Book** — Two sides of resting buy and sell orders. The highest buy price is the best bid, and the lowest sell price is the best ask.

2. **Event** — Something that happens in the market and is represented as data, such as a trade, an order-book update, or a snapshot.

3. **Bid** — An offer to buy an asset. The highest bid is the best bid.

4. **Ask** — An offer to sell an asset. The lowest ask is the best ask.

5. **Spread** — The difference between the best ask and the best bid.

6. **Tick** — The smallest permitted price increment for an asset. In some contexts, it can also mean an individual market-data update.

7. **L1 Data** — Top-of-book data, typically showing the best bid and ask prices and quantities, along with recent trades depending on the feed.

8. **L2 Data** — Market data showing aggregated quantities at multiple price levels on the bid and ask sides.

9. **OBI (Order Book Imbalance)** — A measure of the difference between bid-side and ask-side quantities relative to their combined quantity. It can help estimate short-term buying or selling pressure.

10. **Microprice** — A weighted estimate of the fair price derived from the best bid, best ask, and quantities available at those prices.

11. **Signal** — An output from a strategy indicating a potential trading action, such as buying, selling, or doing nothing.

12. **Order** — An instruction to buy or sell a specified quantity of an asset under certain conditions.

13. **Fill** — The execution of some or all of an order's quantity.

14. **Slippage** — The difference between the expected execution price and the actual execution price.

15. **Latency** — The time taken between two defined events, such as receiving a market-data update and generating a signal.

16. **Throughput** — The number of events or operations a system processes per unit of time.

17. **Market Order** — An order intended to execute immediately against available liquidity, subject to market conditions.

18. **Limit Order** — An order specifying the maximum price a buyer will pay or the minimum price a seller will accept.

19. **Liquidity** — How easily an asset can be traded without substantially moving its price. In an order book, displayed depth is one indicator of liquidity.

20. **Market Depth** — The quantities available at different price levels on the bid and ask sides.

21. **Mid-price** — The average of the best bid and best ask prices.

22. **Trade** — A completed transaction between a buyer and a seller.

23. **Maker** — A participant whose order adds liquidity to the order book.

24. **Taker** — A participant whose order consumes existing liquidity.

25. **Backtesting** — Evaluating a strategy on historical data to estimate how it would have performed.

26. **Paper Trading** — Simulating trades without placing real orders or risking real capital.

27. **Execution** — The process of submitting orders and handling their acknowledgements, fills, cancellations, and rejections.

28. **Position** — The quantity of an asset currently held, including its long or short direction.

29. **P&L (Profit and Loss)** — The gain or loss generated by trading, including the distinction between realized and unrealized P&L.

30. **Adverse Selection** — The risk of trading against someone whose information or timing advantage causes the market price to move against your position.

31. **Price-Time Priority** — A matching rule in which better-priced orders execute first, followed by earlier orders at the same price.

32. **Snapshot** — A representation of the order book at a particular point in time.

33. **Incremental Update** — A market-data message describing a change to existing market state rather than providing the entire state again.

34. **Replay** — Processing recorded market events in sequence to reproduce system behaviour.

35. **Benchmark** — A controlled measurement of performance, such as throughput, latency, or memory usage.
